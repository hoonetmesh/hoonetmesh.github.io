TRAFFIC REPORT

Highway Congestion? Nah — We're Cruising.

Yesterday’s packet traffic hit 5,359 packets, averaging 223 per hour, a notable drop from Friday’s 6,343 but still above the weekly norm. Traffic peaked at noon, with 303 packets flooding the ether — the busiest hour of the day. Curiously, only 47 nodes were active during that spike, suggesting a few chatty repeaters hogged the lane.

The early morning (6 to 8 a.m.) flowed smoothly with 240–280 packets/hour — the kind of steady pace you’d want on your way to breakfast. But by 8 p.m., things quieted to a crawl with just 118 packets/hour, quieter than a backroad in Rumney.

Channel utilization sat at 13.1%, and airtime at 3.8% — confirming that while traffic is lively, the mesh isn’t even close to gridlock.

  • Average RSSI: -93 dBm (moderate propagation)
  • Strongest Signal: -28 dBm (local node practically on top of us)
  • Weakest Signal: -126 dBm (long-range survivor)

Signal-to-noise ratios were mostly cooperative, averaging -8.1 dB SNR, with peaks at a sunny +8.0 dB and lows at -21.8 dB — a snow squall of RF interference.

Most packets (1,395) arrived in the strong category (≥ -100 dBm), though surprisingly few were tagged as good, possibly due to brief listener lulls. Over 2,400 packets were weak or very weak, suggesting long-range paths or marginal nodes.

Hop counts confirmed the mesh’s depth: - 3 hops was the most common path (730 packets) - 0 hops (direct signal): 98 packets - Max hops: 6 — a full cascade of mesh relay

Takeaway: Yesterday’s RF weather was a mix of clear local skies and distant static storms — good for long-range listening but with plenty of signal fading.
