Signal Conditions

Sunday’s RF skies were partly cloudy—with patches of fog. Our average signal strength sat at -113 dBm, with a wide range from -14 dBm (clear as a bell) down to -128 dBm (fading into static). Think of it as driving through New Hampshire back roads—some turns bring you a crystal-clear signal, others leave you grasping at noise.

The Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) averaged -9.1 dB, ranging from a crisp +7.8 dB to a muddy -21.0 dB. The strongest signal of the day was HOONET-UNCANOONUC, which blazed through with -32 dBm RSSI and +6.7 dB SNR—a digital lighthouse cutting through the fog.

Nearly 75% of packets were received at -120 dBm or weaker, indicating weak signal propagation, likely due to terrain and seasonal atmospheric conditions. Most packets traveled 2–3 hops, with 48 packets making it all the way through 6-hop paths. That’s impressive endurance for weak signals.

In short, propagation remains fair to poor, but the mesh still holds strong—thanks to resilient routing and active repeaters.
