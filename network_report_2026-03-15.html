📻 FRONT PAGE

The New England Meshtastic network rolled into another productive Sunday, logging 562 total nodes, with 251 active in the last 24 hours and 46 online in the last hour. Traffic remained steady with 4,841 packets across the day, averaging 202 per hour, while channel utilization held stable at 10.5%.

A modest but consistent influx of 14 new nodes joined the fray, expanding the network footprint from the Berkshires to coastal Maine. Among the fresh faces were Meshtastic SolarBowler, Mast Road Solar, and DLYMesh 1c64, the latter pushing our eastern edge detection limits.

The mesh remains healthy, growing, and ready for St. Paddy's Day traffic — just keep an eye on those low batteries.

🚦 TRAFFIC REPORT

The digital highways were flowing at a comfortable pace on Sunday. Our 24-hour packet tally came in at 4,841, or about 202 packets per hour, with the busiest hour hitting at 6:00 AM ET when 272 packets and 102 active nodes lit up the mesh.

Traffic dipped at 2:00 PM ET to a sluggish 79 packets, but things picked up again by evening, peaking once more around 9:00 PM with 263 packets. Channel utilization sat at 10.5%, with transmit airtime at 2.4% — meaning plenty of open LoRa lanes.

Packet types were dominated by telemetry (2,027) and position reports (1,363), which makes sense for a network of this size. Text messages were sparse — only 107 — perhaps because everyone’s too busy monitoring their SNR.

We welcomed 14 new nodes, while 10 previous nodes went dark, including HOONET-SLAP and Starbase80. Some may be offline, others simply out of range — the mesh is a fickle friend.

Overall, the traffic looked like a Sunday drive through New England — smooth, scenic, and occasionally slow, but never gridlocked.

📡 SIGNAL CONDITIONS

Signal conditions Sunday were like a partly cloudy sky — bright spots amid the murk. We logged 3,392 packets with signal data, and the average RSSI was -105 dBm, with an SNR of -11.6 dB. For perspective, anything above -100 dBm is considered solid, and we saw 760 packets in that green zone.

However, nearly 1,450 packets fell between -120 and -115 dBm, and over 1,000 packets were weaker than -120 dBm — brushing against the noise floor. Propagation wasn’t ideal, but far from catastrophic.

The star of the show was HOONET-DUNCAN, our own solar-powered repeater. It averaged a clean -47 dBm with an SNR of 5.7 dB — like a beacon on a mountaintop. It’s no surprise that 73 packets arrived directly from DUNCAN, many with full signal bars.

Hop counts revealed a well-connected mesh: - 0 hops: 73 packets - 1 hop: 305 - 2–4 hops: 570–726 each - 5+ hops: 402 packets

So while signals were weaker than usual, the network still delivered reliably, proving once again that multi-hop routing is the name of the game.
